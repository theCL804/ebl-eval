Week 2: Terrifyingly Lucky, Federally Funded, and a Coach on the Hot Seat

The league got its first real personality this week. The Chicken Boys are 2-0 and it should scare everybody, except the margin says otherwise. Cheese Children lost a game their own coaching staff handed away, for the second week running. Nikki's Victims are still riding federal assistance at quarterback. And somehow the two funniest standings lines belong to a 0-2 preseason Contender and a 0-2 preseason Win-Now team who can't find a way to actually win despite scoring like a middle-of-the-pack team.

The Chicken Boys🐔 97.0
Mattpao 96.5

Margin: 0.5

This is how a 2-0 start should feel unstoppable, and instead it feels like a warning shot. Chicken Boys survived by 0.46 points, the width of a single extra point, on a week where nobody in their lineup even cracked 21: Kenneth Walker led them with 20.8, Jaylen Waddle put up 18.8, Chris Olave 18.6, and Justin Herbert only managed 10.88 at QB. Mattpao got a strong receiving day from DeVonta Smith (22.7) and a shocking 18.3 out of Jake Ferguson at tight end, but Caleb Williams gave them nothing resembling a floor start at quarterback, 8.72 points, and that gap at the position was the whole game. A "fallen-off dynasty" going 2-0 is scary on paper. Winning by less than half a point in back-to-back weeks is not the form of a juggernaut, it's the form of a team one bad Sunday from 1-1.

DickTatorMiller 86.9
Brock Hard 67.1

Margin: 19.9

DickTatorMiller is quietly 2-0 and still nobody's talking about it. Travis Kelce led the way with 20.6, Derrick Henry added 16.2, Jayden Daniels chipped in 14.74 at QB, and the only sour note was DJ Moore finishing at -0.1, an actively negative fantasy performance. Brock Hard leaned entirely on Brock Purdy, who put up 29.48, more than 40 percent of the team's entire output, and got almost nothing behind him: Garrett Wilson managed 14.2, but Mike Gesicki scored a flat 0.0 at tight end and the backfield of Jacory Croskey-Merritt (5.3), David Montgomery (3.4) and Rhamondre Stevenson (3.1) combined for under 12 points. That's the week's low score, and it wasn't close to being competitive past Purdy's one big game.

The Cheese Children 84.2
Nikkis’ Victims 89.8

Margin: 5.6

The coaching staff lost this game, not the roster, for the second straight week. Bryce Young sat on the bench again and outscored the starting quarterback again, 25.08 to Baker Mayfield's 14.18, the same exact mistake as week 1 repeated verbatim. On top of that, Puka Nacua started at the dedicated WR slot and scored a flat 0.0, while Blake Corum, an RB, sat unused on the bench with 9.70 points and was flex-eligible the whole time. Just sliding Corum into the flex in place of Nacua would have been worth 9.70 points on its own, enough by itself to flip this loss into a win. James Cook (20.4) and George Kittle (17.0) had genuinely good games and none of it mattered, because between the Young/Mayfield mistake and the Nacua/Corum mistake, this team left well over 30 points unused in a game they lost by 5.64. Two weeks, two losses that were entirely self-inflicted, and the same core mistake at quarterback both times. If this were a real front office, the coach would already be fielding questions. On the other side, Nikki's Victims moved to 1-1 doing it almost entirely on Josh Allen, who put up 43.82, more than double the next-best starter, Omarion Hampton at 16.5. Tee Higgins added 12.0 and everyone else on the roster combined for under 17 points. This team is not winning games so much as collecting a federal benefits check at quarterback every Sunday and calling it a strategy.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba put up 41.0 by himself, nearly half his team's entire output, and it still wasn't enough: Jordan Love added a modest 13.8 at QB and Breece Hall 12.7, but there was nothing else behind Smith-Njigba's monster game. BateBoul got a clean, balanced week instead of one outlier: Ja'Marr Chase (23.0), Jahmyr Gibbs (20.3), Jalen Hurts (18.16) and Kyren Williams (14.7) all contributed, and that's the difference between a roster that needs a superstar game to win and one that doesn't.

The Moon Unit 138.9
The Factory 84.3

Margin: 54.5

The Moon Unit followed a 124.0 week 1 with 138.86 in week 2, and this time it came from two receivers going off in the same lineup: CeeDee Lamb put up 31.3 and Amon-Ra St. Brown 30.7, with Christian McCaffrey (20.6) and Stefon Diggs (19.2) adding on top of that. Through two weeks this is 262.86 points for, the most in the league by roughly 34 over second place, and right now this is the best roster in the league by a real margin, not a narrow one. The Factory, meanwhile, got a solid Tre Tucker game (24.4) and a surprising 20.0 from Dalton Schultz at tight end, and it still wasn't enough behind a 10.02-point Drake Maye start at quarterback. 0-2, and getting exactly the results a team with no real 2026 assets and a rebuild on paper should be getting.

Long Live Wopo 111.8
The Choo Choo Crew 99.6

Margin: 12.2

Choo Choo Crew is 0-2, which might be the funniest thing in the league right now, because it's not for lack of scoring. Jonathan Taylor put up 27.2, Joe Burrow 16.18 at quarterback, and the team's total of 217.24 points through two weeks is 6th in the league, dead in the middle of the pack, ahead of exactly half the other 11 teams. They just haven't found a week where it's enough: they lost by 5.94 in week 1 and by 12.22 here, both times to opponents having a good day rather than to their own bad one. This week that opponent was Long Live Wopo, who got a 36.5-point Davante Adams game and 19.0 from Dalton Kincaid at tight end. A preseason Win-Now Contender sitting at 0-2 with solidly average scoring is the most "2026 is not going to plan" storyline in the league so far.
